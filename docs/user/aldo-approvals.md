# Approvals and heads-ups in Aldo

## Approvals

Agents work in your mail and calendar carefully: they draft rather than send. When an agent has an email ready, or an event for your calendar, it puts it in front of you to approve with one tap, and Aldo does the rest. Before an agent spends your money or commits you to something (buying, booking or reserving with a fee, cancelling, submitting an application, claim or form, or calling someone for you), it asks you the same way and waits for your yes. Asked only to find or check something, it stops there.

Approvals show at the top of the home screen under **Approve**, and on your devices as a notification:

- **An email** an agent drafted in your Gmail or Outlook shows exactly what would go: who it's to (with any Cc and Bcc), what's attached, and its text. **Send** sends the draft as it is; **Discard** deletes it from your drafts.
- **An event** shows when it is, who it invites, and where. **Add** (or **Add and invite**) puts it on your calendar and sends the invitations; **Discard** drops it.
- **A suggestion** from one of Aldo's heads-ups (below) shows what an agent would be asked to do. **Start it** starts a thread for it, which drafts before anything is sent; **Not now** drops it.
- **A step that needs your yes** (marked **Needs your yes**) shows what the agent wants to do, where (the store, site or business, and for a call its number), the total with fees and tax, and the terms: cancellation and refund rules, fees, and what it commits you to. **Buy**, **Book**, **Cancel it**, **Submit** or **Call** tells the agent to go ahead, exactly as shown; **Don't** tells it not to. Either way the agent is told at once, and it takes the step only after you approve. If the price or terms change before it does, it asks you again.

For an email, an event or a suggestion, on a computer or an Android phone the notification has the same two buttons, so you can decide without opening Aldo. A step that needs your yes always opens its card, so you see its terms before you decide. On an iPhone, tap the notification to open the home screen and decide there. You can also tell Aldo: "send the reply to Sam".

**Always allow…** sits on some emails and events: one to a few people you name, or an event with no guests. It approves this one and saves a rule in plain words, like "Always let agents email maya@example.com without asking" or "Always let agents add events to my calendar with no guests". From then on, agents do exactly what the rule covers without asking you. The toast that confirms it has **Undo**, and every rule is listed under **How Aldo works for you** on the home screen, with **Remove**. Steps that need your yes (paying, booking, cancelling, submitting, calling) never offer it: each one is asked about on its own.

If an email's draft changed after you were shown it, it isn't sent: the card shows it again as it is now, for you to look at before you send it. What you decided in the last day stays listed under the cards. An approval you leave for a week expires; an email draft stays in your drafts.

## When an agent needs you in its browser

Some things only you can get past: a check that you're human, a sign-in, a code sent to your phone or email, a number to match, or approving a sign-in on another device. When an agent meets one, it asks you at once and stops, rather than waiting in the chat.

The thread shows a card over its message box, with what it is, about how long it takes, and what to do:

- **Do it now** opens the agent's browser full screen, on the page. Do what it asks (tick the box, sign in), then tap **I'm done**, and the agent carries on from there. It works by touch on a phone.
- **A code**: type it into the card's field and tap **Type it in**. Aldo puts it into the page's code field for you. It's never kept, and the agent never sees it. If the page has no field ready for it, you're told to open the browser and type it there.
- **On another device** (approving a sign-in, tapping a number): do it there, then tap **I'm done**.
- **Skip** tells the agent to go on without that site. It says what it couldn't check.

The same request shows under **Needs you** on the home screen, and comes to your devices as a notification with **Do it now** and **Skip**. While it's open, the Browser panel says "Your agent is waiting for you here", with **Take over**. If you don't answer in time (half an hour, unless the agent said otherwise), the agent is told to go on without it.

## Teaching your agent a task

Instead of explaining a task, you can show it once. **Teach this** is in the toolbar of the agent's browser when you've opened it with **Do it now**, and in the Browser panel's **…** menu. It records you doing the task in the agent's browser:

1. Choose **Teach this**. The browser shows **Recording**, with how long it's run and **Stop**. Agents wait while it records, and it stops by itself after 10 minutes.
2. Do the task, start to finish, as you'd want it done.
3. Choose **Stop**, then say in a sentence what your agent should learn to do, like "Book a table on OpenTable for the time and party size I say".
4. **Teach it** puts a message in the thread's message box, with the steps you took. Read it over and send it. **Discard** throws the recording away.

The message asks the agent to write a skill from it (it shows up in **Settings → Skills**), with what was particular to this time (a date, a name, a party size) as inputs it asks for. If it can do so without committing you to anything, it tries the skill once on another input, stopping before any step that books, buys, sends or submits. Then it tells you what the skill does, and offers to run it as a routine if that would help.

**What it records**: the pages you go to (their address and title), what you click (a button's or link's name), what you type into each field (the final text, with the field's label), what you choose in lists, checkboxes and options, and when you send a form or press Enter. It records only while it says **Recording**, and only your own steps in the agent's browser, in every tab.

**What it never records**: passwords, one-time codes, and card details (the number, the security code, the expiry date). For those the step says only that you typed one, such as "Typed a password into Password". Parts of an address that can carry a sign-in (tokens, codes, keys) are left out, and a value from your vault shows as its name, never its value. Scrolling, hovering, and what you do inside a frame from another site (such as a payment form a store embeds) aren't recorded either.

**Where it goes**: while you record and until you choose, the steps live only in the memory of the thread's computer. A recording never goes into Aldo's logs, events, or database. **Teach it** gives the steps once to your browser, which puts them in the message box; **Discard**, or half an hour without either, deletes them. Once you send the message, it's part of that conversation like anything else you send, and nothing else keeps it.

## Heads-ups

When your Google or Microsoft account is connected, Aldo looks at your new mail and coming events every half hour of your day (7 AM to 9:30 PM where you are) and tells you what's worth knowing: someone who needs an answer soon, a meeting that moved or clashes with another, an invitation you haven't answered. Most looks say nothing. Newsletters, receipts and automated mail are left out, and so is anything that looks like phishing.

A heads-up comes as a message from Aldo in your conversation with it and as a notification. Some come with a suggestion, like drafting a reply, which waits under **Approve** for you to start it.

The first look after you turn heads-ups on only notes what's already there, so nothing old comes up. Turn them off in **Settings → Integrations → Heads-ups from Aldo**, or tell Aldo.
